CV sections: every heading and what goes under it

Nobody uses every section. Choose the headings your field expects, put them in the order your reader cares about, and leave out any that would be empty.

How to use this page

A CV is built from standard sections. Readers in each field expect certain ones and look for them in a familiar place. The table lists the sections in common use, what each contains, and who needs it. After the table are suggested orders for four kinds of CV.

SectionWhat goes in itWho needs it
Contact detailsName, present designation and institution, postal address for correspondence, phone, email, profile linkEveryone
Profile or summaryFour to six lines: field, level, strengths, scopeSenior professionals; optional for academics
Research interestsThree to six topics or a short paragraphAcademics, researchers, PhD applicants
EducationDegrees with subject, university, year, grade; thesis title and supervisor for research degreesEveryone
Eligibility and professional qualificationsEligibility tests, professional examinations, registrations and licences with number and dateTeachers, doctors, lawyers, accountants, engineers
Appointments or career historyPosts held with employer, nature of post and exact datesEveryone with work history
Key achievementsFour to six results with figures, from the whole careerSenior professionals
PublicationsBooks, journal articles, chapters, proceedings, patents, each in full citation formAcademics, researchers, doctors; others if they have any
Conference presentations and invited talksTitle, event, organiser, place, date; mark invited talksAcademics, researchers, doctors
Research projects and grantsTitle, funder, period, role, statusAcademics, scientists
Teaching experienceCourses taught, level, years; course design; examinershipsTeachers
Research supervisionScholars guided: awarded and ongoing, with titlesUniversity teachers
Clinical experiencePostings by department and duration; procedures and case volumes where customaryDoctors, nurses, allied health
Technical skillsLaboratory techniques, instruments, software, programming languagesScientists, engineers, analysts
Awards, fellowships and honoursName of award, awarding body, yearAnyone who has them
Training, workshops and courses attendedTitle, organiser, datesTeachers (orientation and refresher courses), professionals
Academic and professional serviceCommittees, boards of studies, editorial and reviewing work, conferences organisedAcademics, senior professionals
Board and advisory rolesDirectorships, advisory boards, with datesSenior professionals
MembershipsProfessional bodies and learned societies, with grade and yearProfessionals, academics

Suggested orders

Academic or research CV

Contact details; education; eligibility tests; appointments; research interests; publications; conference presentations; projects and grants; teaching; research supervision; awards; academic service; courses attended; memberships; referees. Details on the academic CV page.

Doctor's CV

Contact details and registration; education and training; present post; clinical experience by posting; procedures and skills; publications and presentations; teaching; audits and quality projects; courses and conferences; memberships; referees. See the doctor's CV sample.

Senior professional CV

Contact line; title line; profile; key achievements; career history; board roles; education and professional qualifications; other. Details on the senior professional CV page.

Student applying for higher study

Contact details; education; research experience and projects; publications and presentations if any; internships and work; skills; awards and scholarships; activities; referees. Details on the study abroad CV page.

Naming and consistency

  • Use the plain, expected name for each heading. A committee looking for "Publications" should not have to recognise it under "Scholarly output".
  • Keep the same order of details inside every entry of a section.
  • Use one date form and one citation style throughout.
  • Number long lists.
  • Leave out empty sections; do not write "Nil".

What does not belong in any section

Opinions about yourself, unexplained abbreviations, family history, salary, and anything you would not want checked. Identity numbers such as Aadhaar or PAN are never part of a CV; give them only on official forms.

Questions people ask

Which sections are essential in every CV?

Contact details, education and appointments or career history. The rest depend on your field.

Should I include empty sections with "Nil"?

No. Leave out any section for which you have nothing to list, unless a prescribed form requires every heading.

Where do referees go?

At the end, in academic and research CVs. Senior professional CVs usually omit them.
