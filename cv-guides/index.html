CV guides

9 guides to the full curriculum vitae: the long document used in teaching, research, medicine and senior careers.

  • What a CV is, and who needs one A resume is a selection. A curriculum vitae is the record. If you teach, research, practise medicine or have a long senior career, the record is what you will be asked for.
  • Academic CV format for teaching and research posts A selection committee reads an academic CV with three questions: what have you studied, what have you published, and what have you taught. Make each answer easy to find.
  • How to list publications in a CV A publication list is read by people who know the field and will look some entries up. Its virtues are completeness, consistency and exactness.
  • CV for study abroad: masters and PhD applications An admissions reader is not hiring you. They are asking whether you can do the course, and for a research degree, whether you can do research.
  • Senior professional CV: the two to three page format After fifteen years, one page cannot hold the career and ten pages will not be read. Two or three well-ordered pages are the form senior readers expect.
  • CV sections: every heading and what goes under it Nobody uses every section. Choose the headings your field expects, put them in the order your reader cares about, and leave out any that would be empty.
  • CV length and layout for a multi-page document A long CV is used like a reference book. People do not read it through; they look things up. Lay it out so that looking up is easy.
  • Referees in a CV: whom to name and how A referee lends you their name. Choose people who know your work well, ask them properly, and make their task easy.
  • Resume, CV or biodata: which document do you need? Tell us what you are applying for and the right document follows. Sending the wrong kind is a small mistake that is easy to avoid.
  • CV checklist before you submit Checks for facts, publications, layout and the file.

Start here

If you are not sure whether you need a CV at all, begin with which document do you need, which sorts out resume, CV, biodata and prescribed forms in a minute, and then what a CV is. For most private-sector jobs the answer is a one-page resume, and our neighbour resume.shareyourgroup.com is the place for that.

If you do need the long document, read the guide for your field. Teachers and researchers should read academic CV and then publications, which is the section committees read most closely. Students applying for higher degrees should read CV for study abroad; most of it applies to applications within India as well. Managers and specialists with long careers should read senior professional CV.

Reference pages

CV sections lists every heading in common use, what goes under it and who needs it, with a suggested order for four kinds of CV. Length and layout deals with what makes a many-page document easy to use: running headers, page numbers, aligned dates, numbering. Referees explains whom to ask, how to ask, and how to list them.

What these guides do not do

They do not reproduce the regulations, score sheets or application forms of any university, commission or council. Those are issued by the bodies themselves and change from year to year. Use the body's own current documents, and use your CV as the source of facts for filling them in.

See it applied

The sample CVs show four complete documents with notes: an assistant professor applicant, a PhD applicant, a doctor and a senior manager.

General guidance. This page gives general suggestions, reviewed in November 2026. If an institution, employer or country gives its own form, format or instruction, follow that. Every person, institution and publication in our examples is invented. Nothing here is legal, immigration or professional advice.
