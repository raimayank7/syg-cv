Sample CVs

Four complete curricula vitae for the fields that use them, each with notes on why it is set out as it is and how to adapt it.

  • Academic CV sample (assistant professor applicant) For candidates applying for teaching posts in colleges and universities.
  • CV sample for a PhD or research fellowship application For postgraduate students applying for a doctoral programme, a project fellowship or a research assistant post.
  • Doctor's CV sample (medical CV) For doctors applying for senior resident, registrar, consultant or fellowship posts.
  • Senior manager CV sample (two page executive format) For general managers, vice presidents and function heads approached by search firms or applying for leadership roles.

How to read a sample CV

A sample shows structure, not content. The people in these four documents do not exist, and the journals, colleges and companies are placeholders in square brackets. What is real is the arrangement: which sections appear, in what order, what each entry contains, and how unfinished or uncertain items are labelled. Those are the things a selection committee responds to, and they carry over to your own field even if it is not botany, chemistry, medicine or manufacturing.

Read the notes under each sample before the sample itself. They say what a committee looks for in that kind of CV and where candidates commonly lose its confidence.

Which sample is nearest to you

  • Applying for a teaching post, or already teaching: the academic CV.
  • Finishing a master's degree and applying for a PhD or fellowship: the PhD applicant CV.
  • A doctor, nurse or other clinician: the doctor's CV, for its treatment of registration, postings and procedures.
  • A manager or specialist with fifteen or more years of experience: the senior manager CV.

Do not copy the facts

It should not need saying, but every year candidates submit CVs with sample text left in. Replace every bracket, check every date against your own certificates, and remove any line for which you have nothing true to write. A shorter honest CV is always stronger than a longer one with borrowed entries.
