Academic CV sample (assistant professor applicant)

For candidates applying for teaching posts in colleges and universities. The sample shows a lecturer about six years after her doctorate: the sections a selection committee expects, in the order it looks for them.

Every name, institution, journal and paper in this sample is invented. Square brackets mark where your own real details go.

Why it is set out this way

  • Eligibility has its own heading straight after education, because committees check it first.
  • Each appointment states its nature: temporary, guest, fellowship. Experience is counted by nature and duration, so precision matters.
  • Publications are divided by type and numbered straight through, with a summary line of counts that matches the list.
  • Work under review is separated and labelled, never mixed with published papers.
  • Teaching lists actual papers and levels, with dissertations guided, showing readiness to carry a timetable.
  • Service to the college (examinations, herbarium, NSS) shows a colleague who shares the institution's work.

Adapting it for yourself

  • Use the citation style of your own discipline and list every publication in full; the sample shortens the list only to save space.
  • Put teaching before publications if you are applying to a college where teaching is the main duty.
  • Give exact dates for every appointment and keep the appointment letters ready.
  • If the institution issues its own form or score sheet, fill that; use this CV as your source.
  • Name referees only with their consent, with official email addresses.

Questions people ask

How many pages will an academic CV like this be?

With all publications written out in full, about three to four pages. That is normal; do not shorten it by removing true entries.

Should I include papers that are under review?

Yes, under a separate, clearly labelled heading.
