CV sample for a PhD or research fellowship application

For postgraduate students applying for a doctoral programme, a project fellowship or a research assistant post. With no publications yet, the CV rests on the dissertation, laboratory or field skills and academic record.

The sample

Arif Hussain

[Address] | [Phone with country code] | [Email]

Research interests

Water quality and public health; low-cost methods of water testing; environmental chemistry.

Education

M.Sc., Chemistry (Analytical), [University], 2026, 8.4/10 CGPA, third in a class of 46

B.Sc. (Hons.), Chemistry, [College, University], 2024, 79%

Eligibility

[National or state eligibility or entrance test], Chemical Sciences, [month and year], qualified for [fellowship or lectureship].

Research experience

M.Sc. dissertation: "Fluoride in groundwater of eight villages of [District]: levels and seasonal change"
Supervisor: Dr [Name], Department of Chemistry, [University]. July 2025 to April 2026.

  • Collected 96 samples from hand pumps and borewells in pre-monsoon and post-monsoon seasons
  • Measured fluoride by ion-selective electrode and checked a subset by a colorimetric method
  • Found 11 sources above the acceptable limit used in the study; mapped them and reported to the supervisor's project team
  • Wrote a 60 page dissertation, graded A

Summer research internship, [Institute], May to June 2025, under Dr [Name]

  • Prepared standards and ran calibration for metal analysis on an atomic absorption spectrophotometer
  • Analysed 40 soil extracts for lead and cadmium

Presentations

"Seasonal change in groundwater fluoride", poster, Research Scholars' Day, [University], March 2026.

Technical skills

Instruments: UV-visible spectrophotometer, ion-selective electrodes, atomic absorption spectrophotometer (supervised use), pH and conductivity meters
Methods: titrimetry, sample preservation, calibration, basic quality control
Software: spreadsheet statistics and charts; basic R; reference manager

Relevant courses

Instrumental methods of analysis; Environmental chemistry; Statistics for chemists; Research methodology.

Awards and scholarships

Merit scholarship, [University], 2024 to 2026. First prize, inter-college science quiz, 2023.

Activities

Volunteer, department's water-testing camp for two villages, 2025. Student coordinator, Chemistry Association, 2025-26.

Languages

English, Hindi, Urdu.

Referees

Dr [Name], Associate Professor, Department of Chemistry, [University] (dissertation supervisor). [Official email]
Prof. [Name], Head, Department of Chemistry, [University]. [Official email]

Every name, institution, journal and paper in this sample is invented. Square brackets mark where your own real details go.

Why it is set out this way

  • Research interests come first and are specific, so a prospective supervisor can see the fit at once.
  • The dissertation is described as a piece of research: question, samples, method, finding, output.
  • Instruments are named with an honest note of the level of use.
  • Rank in class and a merit scholarship support the grade.
  • Referees are the two teachers who know the work best, with official email addresses.

Adapting it for yourself

  • Replace the dissertation with your own, keeping the four points: what you asked, what you did, how, and what came of it.
  • If you have a paper submitted or published, add a Publications heading above Presentations.
  • Match the research interests, truthfully, to the department or project you are applying to.
  • For applications abroad, give grades with their scale and follow the university's page limit.

Questions people ask

I have no publications. Can I still apply for a PhD?

Yes. At entry, departments look at your academic record, dissertation or project work, skills and referees' letters.

How long should this CV be?

About two pages.
