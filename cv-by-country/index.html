CV and resume by country

What official careers services in six places say about the job document: what it is called, how long it is, and which personal details belong on it.

  • CV for the United Kingdom In the UK the short job document is called a CV, not a resume, and it leaves out most of the personal details an Indian biodata begins with.
  • Resume for Canada Canada calls it a resume, wants no photograph, and is firm that personal details stay off the page.
  • Resume for the United States In the United States the job document is a resume, the full street address has dropped away, and a headline under the name does the work of an Indian "objective".
  • CV for Germany (Lebenslauf) Germany is the exception among these six places: date of birth is included, a photograph is still widely expected, and the CV ends with a date and signature.
  • Europass CV: the European Union's CV format Europass is not a country's custom but a common format offered by the European Union, with an online builder that produces the CV for you.
  • Resume for Australia Australia uses "resume" and "CV" for the same document, allows it to run to two or three pages, and expects no personal particulars.

What the table shows

Two things stand out for an applicant from India. First, in the English-speaking countries the personal particulars that open a traditional Indian biodata (date of birth, marital status, father's name, photograph) are either expressly discouraged or simply absent from the official lists. The reason given by Canada's Job Bank is to prevent potential bias. Second, Germany is different: the Federal Employment Agency's guidance includes date and place of birth, says most employers still expect a photograph, and ends the CV with a date and signature.

So there is no single "international format". Before applying, find out what is customary where the employer is, and above all read what the employer itself asks for. An employer's own instruction outranks any general custom.

Things that are the same everywhere

  • Latest experience first.
  • Tailor the document to the job or course.
  • Be truthful; claims are checked.
  • Keep it clear and consistent, and have someone else proofread it.
  • Explain Indian degrees, grades, examinations and employers in a few words for a reader who does not know them.
  • Write dates with the month in words. "03/04/2025" means 3 April in some countries and 4 March in others.
  • Give phone numbers with the country code.

What we did not cover

We have included only places for which we could read an official careers page ourselves. Countries of the Gulf, Singapore, New Zealand and others are not here yet, because we would rather leave a page out than write it from hearsay. For any country, the careers service of its government or the employer's own website is the place to check.

Questions people ask

Which countries expect a photo on a CV?

Of the six sources we read, only the German Federal Employment Agency said a photo is still expected by most employers, though not mandatory. Canada's Job Bank says a photo is not the norm there.

Is "CV" or "resume" the right word abroad?

It depends on the place: CV in the United Kingdom and Germany, resume in the United States and Canada, and either word in Australia.

Do these pages cover visas and work permits?

No. They cover only how the document is written. Visas, work rights and recognition of qualifications have their own official processes.

General guidance. This page gives general suggestions, reviewed in November 2026. If an institution, employer or country gives its own form, format or instruction, follow that.
