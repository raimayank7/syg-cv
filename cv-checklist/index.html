CV checklist before you submit

23 checks for a full curriculum vitae. Open your CV beside this page and tick each line only after looking.

How a CV goes wrong

A long CV rarely fails for lack of achievement. It fails on small things that make a committee doubt the rest: a degree year that does not match the certificate, a paper listed as published that is still under review, two dates that overlap, a publication counted twice. Selection committees and screening staff check documents line by line, and a single inconsistency can cost marks or raise a question at interview. The checks below are grouped in the order a careful reader would apply them: are the facts right, is the scholarly record honestly presented, can things be found, does the document fit this application, and is the file itself in order.

Do the first group with your certificates and appointment letters physically in front of you. Memory is not good enough for years and percentages.

Keep the evidence together

For every line of a CV there should be a document: a degree certificate, an appointment letter, the first page of a paper, an acceptance letter, a sanction order, a participation certificate. Keep scans in one folder, named and ordered like the CV. When an application asks for "self-attested copies of all testimonials", the work is then ten minutes instead of two days.

Questions people ask

Why does a CV need a separate checklist from a resume?

A CV is judged on completeness and exactness by people who can check each entry. The risks are different: a wrong year, a mislabelled paper, a missing page number.

Are my ticks saved?

No. The list starts fresh each time so that you check again for each application.
