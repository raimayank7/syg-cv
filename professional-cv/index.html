Senior professional CV: the two to three page format

After fifteen years, one page cannot hold the career and ten pages will not be read. Two or three well-ordered pages are the form senior readers expect.

When this format applies

A manager or specialist with long experience, applying for senior roles through a search firm, a board, a promoter or a public body, is usually asked for a fuller document than a one-page resume. It is still a selling document, written for a purpose, but it must show the whole arc: growing responsibility, scale, and results at each stage. Two pages suit most; three if the career is long or includes board roles, publications or patents.

Page one decides

Readers at this level skim. The first page should let someone understand who you are and why you matter without turning over.

  1. Name and contact line. Phone, email, city, a professional profile link.
  2. Title line: your level and field. "Chief Financial Officer | Manufacturing | 22 years".
  3. Profile: four to six lines. Scope you have handled (revenue, people, plants, geographies), your main strengths, and the kind of situation you are best in: growth, turnaround, new ventures, compliance.
  4. Key achievements: four to six points drawn from the whole career, each with a number.
  5. Current role, begun on page one.

Career history

Latest first. For each employer give the company with one line saying what it is and its size, then each role held, with dates.

[Company], Pune. Auto components manufacturer; four plants; revenue about ₹1,200 crore.

Vice President, Operations (Apr 2019 to present). Report to the Managing Director; 1,400 employees through six direct reports.

  • Raised on-time delivery from 82% to 96% in two years by rebuilding production planning
  • Commissioned a new plant in 14 months, two months ahead of plan and within budget
  • Cut customer rejections by 60% over three years

General Manager, Plant Head (Jun 2014 to Mar 2019)

  • Led a 450 person plant; doubled output without adding floor space

Give most space to the last ten years. Earlier roles can be one or two lines each, and the first few jobs a single line together: "Earlier: engineer and manager roles at [two companies], 2001 to 2008".

Write scope before results

A senior reader first wants to know the size of the chair you sat in: reporting line, team, budget, revenue, sites. Then the two or three things that changed under you. Leave out duties that go without saying at your level.

Other sections

  • Board and committee roles: directorships, audit or risk committees, industry bodies.
  • Education and professional qualifications: degrees, institutions, years; professional memberships with year of admission.
  • Executive education and certifications that carry weight.
  • Publications, patents, speaking, where relevant, kept short.
  • Languages, and countries worked in.

What to leave out

  • School marks and early-career detail.
  • Lists of routine responsibilities.
  • Every training programme attended.
  • Personal particulars beyond contact details, unless a regulator's form requires them.
  • Compensation. Search firms will ask separately.
  • Confidential figures of your present employer. Use ranges, percentages and publicly reported numbers.

Different versions

Keep a complete master record. From it, prepare the two to three page CV for search firms, a one page summary for introductions, and a formal profile of a few paragraphs for board papers and conference programmes. If a regulator or public body prescribes a format for a "fit and proper" declaration or an application, complete that as asked.

Tone

Plain and factual. At senior levels, large claims in inflated language read as insecurity; exact figures calmly stated read as authority. Use the first person without the pronoun ("Led", "Built"), past tense for past roles, and the same date form throughout.

Layout

Name and page number on each page; a single column; a readable font at 10.5 to 11 point; clear headings. Guidance for multi-page documents is on the length and layout page, and a full invented example is on the senior manager CV sample page.

References

Do not list referees on the document. At this level, references are taken with care and by agreement, late in the process; tell the search firm whom they may approach and when.

Questions people ask

How long should a senior executive CV be?

Two pages for most, three for a long career or one with board roles and publications.

Should I include jobs from twenty years ago?

Yes, but briefly: one line each, or a single line covering the early years.

Do I state my current compensation?

Not on the CV. It is discussed separately with the search firm or employer.
